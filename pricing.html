How the commercial model works

The final contract is custom. The structure is simple: ChainMore platform fee for the operating layer, plus provider costs passed through at cost.

ChainMore platform fee

The commercial fee for making complex rail choices feel like one checkout, one settlement path, and one merchant record.

Provider pass-through

Card, banking, FX, gas, issuer, on/off-ramp, and other rail costs are passed through at cost unless a signed enterprise contract bundles them differently.

Custom operating terms

Support targets, escalation, KYB posture, reporting, audit cooperation, and service terms are agreed around the merchant's real flow.

Pricing questions

Things prospects ask before they sign.

Why do you not publish fixed plans?

Because the economics depend on the complexity ChainMore removes: payment steps, failed attempts, support work, reconciliation load, rails, regions, risk profile, settlement outcome, and volume. A generic public number would either underprice complex merchants or mislead simple ones.

What do you quote?

We quote the ChainMore platform layer: checkout abstraction, routing, orchestration, reconciliation, monitoring, and operating support. The final proposal can include a monthly platform fee, transaction-based platform fee, setup work, support terms, and custom commercial protections.

Are PSP and blockchain fees included?

No, those costs are passed through at cost on a separate line of the invoice. ChainMore does not mark them up. You can see the per-transaction cost breakdown in the merchant dashboard.

Do you support high-volume or regulated businesses?

Yes, but those conversations are handled directly. We review the merchant profile, jurisdictions, settlement model, and provider requirements before proposing commercial terms.

Can we test before a full production launch?

Yes. We can structure sandbox access and a controlled pilot around your target flow, subject to KYB, provider availability, and the commercial agreement.

How fast can we get a proposal?

Send monthly volume, regions, current payment setup, and desired settlement outcome. For qualified merchants we can usually turn that into a first commercial proposal quickly, then refine it after KYB and provider review.

What is required for onboarding?

A signed Main Agreement, a Data Processing Agreement (GDPR Article 28), and basic KYB on the merchant entity. The DPA template, TOMs, and sub-processor list are sent on request before the Main Agreement is signed so your counsel can review them in parallel. DPA details here.

What should we send before the call?

Company name, website, target countries, estimated monthly volume, current payment stack, expected settlement preferences, and the main payment problem you want ChainMore to remove.

Ready to price the flow you want to remove?

Tell us what your customers do today, how you want to receive, and where payments get messy. We will shape the commercial model around that reality.
